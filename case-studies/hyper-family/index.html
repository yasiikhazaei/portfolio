01 · Context

Shopping existed. Loyalty did not.

Hyper Family had no active digital loyalty experience. The main customer touchpoints were phone-number registration at checkout and SMS messages outside the store.

The brief was broad—points, coupons, missions, festivals, rewards, branches, products, payment, and CRM. Before shaping features, I needed to understand the existing shopping behavior they would have to fit.

Research questionWhat actually drives a shopping decision—and where could loyalty add value without adding effort?

02 · Research

Start with shopping, not loyalty.

I combined desk research and loyalty benchmarking with eight contextual interviews inside Hyper Family stores. The work moved from behavior to patterns, then from patterns to product opportunities.

03 · Journey

Shopping already had its own rhythm.

The synthesis revealed a six-stage journey. Loyalty was absent during planning, weak during store choice, occasionally relevant around offers, and easy to forget after the trip.

04 · Synthesis

Value changes with the moment.

01

Routine is not one context

Customers return on a rhythm, but every trip can carry a different purpose, basket, and priority.

02

Value is broader than price

Proximity, assortment, quality, payment conditions, and household needs matter alongside price.

03

Discounts need relevance

A discount becomes meaningful when it connects to a current need, intent, or shopping situation.

04

Loyalty sits outside the routine

None of the participants had used store coupons or points; messages alone did not keep benefits top of mind.

The opportunity is not simply to offer more rewards, but to make loyalty relevant within the way customers already shop.
01

Fit the routine

Make loyalty useful without creating a new habit.

02

Show broader value

Communicate more than price reductions alone.

03

Match the context

Surface the right benefit for the current intent.

04

Enter the moment

Make benefits visible and usable while shopping.

06 · Experience principles

Design around real shopping behavior.

No new habit

Fit into behavior that already exists.

Relevant in the moment

Surface value when it can change a decision.

Effortless to use

Minimize search, memory, and extra steps.

Build continuity

Connect one shopping trip to the next.
